civilian_utility_vehicle_hull_descriptions.txt
(Plain-English hull-block silhouettes for Codex. No mobility parts: NO wheels/axles/tires/tracks/treads/skis.)

Conventions:
- “Vehicle length” = front bumper to rear-most body (include trailer if present).
- “Front/middle/rear third” are rough placement guides.
- Keep everything blocky: boxes, wedges, triangular prisms, cylinders. No high-poly.
- Each class: 1) silhouette, 2) hull blocks front→back (≤5), 3) distinct cues.

=====================================================================
1) Civilian automobile compact (15:16:01:01)

SILHOUETTE
- Front: short nose/hood, steep windshield.
- Side: short overall length; cabin looks “big” relative to car; rear is either hatchback or very short trunk.
- Rear: fairly vertical hatch or stubby rear face.

HULL BLOCKS (front → back)
1) Hood wedge (engine bay):
   - A low wedge occupying roughly the front ~20–25% of length.
   - Slightly lower than the cabin roof; top surface slopes up toward windshield.
2) Cabin block (greenhouse + roof):
   - A box occupying roughly the next ~45–55% of length.
   - Taller than the hood; roof is fairly flat.
3) Rear hatch/stub block:
   - A short box occupying the rear ~20–30% of length.
   - Rear face is near-vertical; height lower than the cabin roof but higher than the hood.

DISTINCT CUES
- Cabin dominates the vehicle length more than midsize/sedan.
- Rear overhang is minimal; hatchback feel (vertical-ish rear).
- Overall “tight” proportions: short hood + short tail.

=====================================================================
2) Civilian automobile midsize (15:16:01:02)

SILHOUETTE
- Front: moderate hood; windshield less steep than compact.
- Side: balanced proportions; not a strong “three-box” like a sedan.
- Rear: modest trunk or fastback.

HULL BLOCKS
1) Hood wedge:
   - Front ~25–30% of length, slightly longer than compact’s hood.
2) Cabin block:
   - Middle ~40–50% of length, roof flatter/longer than compact.
3) Rear deck block:
   - Rear ~20–30% of length; trunk exists but is not long/pronounced.

DISTINCT CUES
- Longer hood than compact; less distinct trunk than sedan.
- “One-and-a-half box” look: hood + cabin with a modest rear volume.

=====================================================================
3) Civilian automobile sedan (15:16:01:03)

SILHOUETTE
- Front: moderate hood, clearly separate from cabin.
- Side: classic three-box profile (hood + cabin + trunk).
- Rear: pronounced trunk; rear face fairly vertical.

HULL BLOCKS
1) Hood wedge:
   - Front ~25–30% of length; low wedge that rises to windshield.
2) Cabin block:
   - Middle ~35–45% of length; roof slightly arched but keep boxy.
3) Trunk block:
   - Rear ~25–35% of length; noticeably “separate” volume from cabin.
   - Lower than cabin roof, but longer than midsize’s rear deck.

DISTINCT CUES
- Strongest three-box silhouette among the cars.
- Trunk is longer and more distinct than midsize.

=====================================================================
4) Civilian pickup / open-bed truck (15:16:02:01 / 02 / 03)
(Three variants: light, medium, heavy. Same basic shape; scale bed length + cab size.)

BASE SILHOUETTE (all pickups)
- Front: pickup hood + upright cab.
- Side: cab in front, open cargo bed behind (low sidewalls).
- Rear: flat tailgate plane.

BASE HULL BLOCKS (front → back)
1) Hood wedge:
   - Front ~20–25% of length; taller/more blunt than a car hood.
2) Cab block:
   - Next ~25–35% of length; tall box with flat roof.
3) Open bed tub:
   - Rear ~40–55% of length; low-height rectangular volume.
   - Key: bed block is LOW compared to cab (reads as “open”).
   - Optionally add a thin “bed-rail lip” (very thin strip) if you need clarity, but count it as part of the bed block, not a new block.

LIGHT (15:16:02:01)
- Shorter bed; cab feels relatively large.
- Bed is ~40–45% of length.

MEDIUM (15:16:02:02)
- Balanced; bed roughly ~45–50% of length.

HEAVY (15:16:02:03)
- Longer bed; sometimes “crew cab” (cab slightly longer).
- Bed up to ~50–55% of length; overall vehicle reads bulkier/taller.

DISTINCT CUES
- The bed volume is low and long, immediately behind the cab.
- Cab is boxier and taller than sedans/SUVs; silhouette shouts “pickup”.

=====================================================================
5) Civilian van (15:16:03:01)

SILHOUETTE
- Front: very short hood/nose (or almost flat), steep windshield.
- Side: tall rectangular body, long continuous roof.
- Rear: vertical rear doors (flat rear face).

HULL BLOCKS
1) Short hood wedge (or front cap box):
   - Front ~10–20% of length; minimal slope; blunt.
2) Main body box:
   - Middle+rear ~70–85% of length; tall rectangular prism.
   - Roof is high and flat; sides are near-vertical.
3) Optional: roof “AC hump” (only if you need differentiation from other vans):
   - A thin box on the roof in the rear half (but keep total blocks ≤5; skip if unnecessary).

DISTINCT CUES
- Tall, single-volume “loaf of bread” profile.
- Rear face is tall and vertical.

=====================================================================
6) Civilian small bus (15:16:03:02)
(Think shuttle / minibus / cutaway bus.)

SILHOUETTE
- Front: van/truck-like nose with tall windshield.
- Side: extended tall passenger box, windows implied by flat sides.
- Rear: vertical rear face, slightly taller than a van.

HULL BLOCKS
1) Front nose/hood wedge:
   - Front ~15–25% of length; slightly longer than a van’s nose.
2) Passenger cabin box:
   - The main tall box, ~60–75% of length; roof high and flat.
3) Optional rear roof cap:
   - A small box at the rear top (rear ~15% of length) to suggest HVAC/raised rear section, if needed.

DISTINCT CUES
- Taller and longer than a van, but not as long as a full city bus.
- Looks like “van front + bus box”.

=====================================================================
7) Civilian large bus (15:16:03:03)
(Think 35–40 ft transit coach profile, big rectangular body.)

SILHOUETTE
- Front: wide blunt face, slightly rounded corners.
- Side: very long rectangular body with a flat roofline.
- Rear: vertical rear face; sometimes a slight rear cap.

HULL BLOCKS
1) Front cap box (driver area):
   - Front ~15–20% of length; full width, tall, blunt.
2) Main passenger body box:
   - Middle ~60–70% of length; long tall rectangular prism.
3) Rear cap box:
   - Rear ~10–20% of length; similar height; can be slightly tapered or slightly shorter to imply engine compartment.

(If you need a 4th block, make it a thin roof “equipment strip” running along the roofline, but only if you have block budget.)

DISTINCT CUES
- Longest “single rigid body” in the civilian set (excluding tractor-trailers).
- Full width, tall, long, and very rectangular.

=====================================================================
8) Civilian SUV (15:16:04:01)

SILHOUETTE
- Front: car-like hood but taller overall stance.
- Side: two-box look: hood + tall cabin/cargo volume; rear is not a separate trunk box like a sedan.
- Rear: near-vertical hatch, larger rear cargo volume.

HULL BLOCKS
1) Hood wedge:
   - Front ~20–25% of length; higher than sedan hood.
2) Cabin/cargo box:
   - Middle+rear ~60–70% of length; tall box.
   - Roof flat; rear quarter feels “bulky”.
3) Rear hatch cap (optional):
   - A small rear box or wedge occupying last ~10–15% to emphasize vertical hatch.

DISTINCT CUES
- Taller cabin than sedan/midsize, with a “single cabin+cargo” volume.
- Rear is hatch-like rather than a long separate trunk.

=====================================================================
9) Civilian small box truck (15:16:04:02)
(Think local delivery truck / step-van-ish or short box truck.)

SILHOUETTE
- Front: small cab, often blunt.
- Side: a rectangular cargo box immediately behind cab; shorter overall length than large box truck.
- Rear: vertical cargo door face.

HULL BLOCKS
1) Cab block:
   - Front ~20–30% of length; tall-ish and blunt, but narrower than the cargo box if you want “cab-forward” feel.
2) Cargo box:
   - Rear ~60–75% of length; tall rectangular prism (the dominant volume).
3) Optional “box collar” (thin strip) at the front of the cargo box to suggest a seam between cab and box (do not count as a new block if possible; treat as a detail of the cargo box).

DISTINCT CUES
- Dominant rectangular cargo volume; clearly not a bus (no long smooth one-piece body).
- Shorter cargo box than large box truck.

=====================================================================
10) Civilian large box truck (15:16:04:03)
(Think 24–26 ft moving truck / big delivery box.)

SILHOUETTE
- Front: cab, moderate length.
- Side: very long tall cargo box; box dominates more than in the small variant.
- Rear: large flat rear door face.

HULL BLOCKS
1) Cab block (or cab + short hood wedge):
   - Front ~15–25% of length; slightly more “truck-like” than small box.
2) Long cargo box:
   - Rear ~70–85% of length; tall rectangular prism.
3) Optional roof fairing wedge (above cab front edge) if you want more detail, but only if within block limit.

DISTINCT CUES
- Cargo box is longer than small box truck and often taller.
- The “box” volume overwhelms the cab visually.

=====================================================================
11) Jeep-type light / medium / heavy (15:16:05:01 / 02 / 03)
(These read more rugged/squared than generic SUVs. Think upright windshield, flat-ish roof, short overhangs.)

BASE SILHOUETTE
- Front: short blunt hood, very upright windshield.
- Side: boxy cabin; roof flat; body looks like a rectangle on top of a rectangle.
- Rear: vertical tailgate; compact rear overhang.

BASE HULL BLOCKS
1) Hood wedge (short, blunt):
   - Front ~15–20% of length; higher and more squared than car hood.
2) Cabin box:
   - Middle ~55–65% of length; upright sides; flat roof.
3) Rear tailgate block:
   - Rear ~15–25% of length; near-vertical rear face.

LIGHT (15:16:05:01)
- Shortest; cabin not very long (2-door vibe).
- Hood and tail are short; “stubby” overall.

MEDIUM (15:16:05:02)
- Slightly longer cabin (4-door vibe); more balanced.

HEAVY (15:16:05:03)
- Longest and tallest; cabin length increases; rear cargo area larger.
- Reads almost like a large off-road SUV, still boxier than normal SUV class.

DISTINCT CUES
- More upright and boxy than the generic SUV class.
- Short overhangs; “rugged cube” look.

=====================================================================
12) Tractor trailer (boxed) light / medium / heavy (15:16:06:01 / 02 / 03)
(Tractor + rectangular enclosed trailer (“dry van” feel).)

BASE SILHOUETTE
- Front: aerodynamic-ish tractor nose; tall cab; (optionally) sleeper behind cab.
- Side: long rectangular trailer box dominates length.
- Rear: tall flat trailer door face.

BASE HULL BLOCKS (front → back)
1) Tractor nose wedge:
   - Front ~8–12% of total combo length; sloped top, blunt face.
2) Cab + sleeper box:
   - Next ~10–18% of combo length; tall rectangular prism behind the nose.
   - Sleeper can be implied as rear half of this cab block (no extra block needed).
3) Fifth-wheel gap / neck region (optional detail, not a block if possible):
   - Just a visual seam between tractor and trailer.
4) Trailer box:
   - Rear ~65–80% of combo length; tall long rectangular prism.
   - Height is near constant; corners slightly rounded if you want.

LIGHT (15:16:06:01)
- Shorter trailer (think “pup” or shorter box); total length noticeably shorter.

MEDIUM (15:16:06:02)
- Standard trailer length, “normal” proportions.

HEAVY (15:16:06:03)
- Longest trailer (common 48–53 ft feel); trailer dominates even more.
- Tractor may feel slightly longer/taller (heavier-duty look).

DISTINCT CUES
- Trailer is a single long tall box: unmistakable “dry van” silhouette.
- Very different from box trucks because the trailer starts after a clear tractor.

=====================================================================
13) Tractor trailer (flatbed) light / medium / heavy (15:16:07:01 / 02 / 03)
(Tractor + long flat deck trailer; deck is low and thin.)

BASE SILHOUETTE
- Front: same tractor as boxed.
- Side: long low deck; open top (no tall trailer walls).
- Rear: thin deck tail; sometimes a headboard at the front of the deck.

BASE HULL BLOCKS
1) Tractor nose wedge.
2) Cab + sleeper box.
3) Flatbed deck slab:
   - Rear ~65–80% of total combo length.
   - Very low height (a thin rectangular prism).
4) Headboard / bulkhead (optional, can be part of deck block):
   - A short vertical plate at the front of the deck (immediately behind tractor).

LIGHT / MEDIUM / HEAVY
- Same scaling logic as boxed: deck length grows across variants.

DISTINCT CUES
- The towed section is LOW and THIN (deck), not a tall rectangular box.

=====================================================================
14) Utility semi-trailer truck classes (15:14:06:01 / 02 / 03)
(Note: “utility” subclasses can vary by your dictionary. These are three common utility trailer silhouettes you can map to 01/02/03 as needed.)

14A) Utility semi-trailer – TANKER-style (suggest mapping to 15:14:06:01)
SILHOUETTE
- Tractor in front; towed trailer is a long horizontal cylinder (rounded ends).

HULL BLOCKS
1) Tractor nose wedge.
2) Cab + sleeper box.
3) Tank cylinder:
   - Rear majority of length; long cylinder lying lengthwise.
   - Add tiny box “end cap” only if needed; prefer cylinder alone.

CUES
- Towed unit is ROUND, not boxy.

14B) Utility semi-trailer – DUMP / TIPPER-style (suggest mapping to 15:14:06:02)
SILHOUETTE
- Trailer looks like a big box with a slanted top edge (dump bed).

HULL BLOCKS
1) Tractor nose wedge.
2) Cab + sleeper box.
3) Dump bed box/wedge:
   - A tall rectangular prism with a slight wedge top or angled rear.
   - Looks heavier and more “industrial” than a dry van.

CUES
- Trailer top edge is sloped/angled; looks like it could tip.

14C) Utility semi-trailer – LOWBOY / EQUIPMENT TRANSPORT (suggest mapping to 15:14:06:03)
SILHOUETTE
- Trailer deck sits very low; often has a raised “gooseneck” at the front.

HULL BLOCKS
1) Tractor nose wedge.
2) Cab + sleeper box.
3) Gooseneck/neck block:
   - A medium-height box immediately behind tractor (front of trailer).
4) Low deck slab:
   - Long thin slab extending rearward; very low height.

CUES
- Trailer is mostly a LOW thin deck with a distinct raised neck at the front.

15) Utility tow truck light / heavy (15:14:12:01 / 02)
(Key feature: rear towing apparatus + boom housing; do NOT model wheels.)

LIGHT TOW (15:14:12:01)
SILHOUETTE
- Front: pickup/medium-truck cab.
- Middle: short equipment body (toolboxes).
- Rear: small boom / wheel-lift assembly.

HULL BLOCKS
1) Hood wedge + cab block (can be 2 blocks if you need: hood wedge then cab box).
2) Equipment body box:
   - A medium-height box behind cab (tool compartments).
3) Rear tow apparatus block:
   - A small angled “A-frame” or wedge at the rear (the wheel-lift mast / stinger housing).
   - Keep it compact.

CUES
- Looks like a pickup/medium truck with a rear “towing mechanism bump”.

HEAVY TOW (15:14:12:02)
SILHOUETTE
- Bigger cab; longer/heavier equipment body.
- Rear: large boom housing, often taller than the body.

HULL BLOCKS
1) Hood wedge.
2) Cab box (taller, more squared).
3) Long equipment body box (tool compartments, ballast).
4) Boom housing block:
   - A tall box or triangular prism rising from the rear half of the body.
5) Rear tow apparatus block:
   - A chunky wedge/box at the rear representing the heavy wheel-lift assembly.

CUES
- Big rear superstructure/boom: unmistakably a heavy wrecker profile.

16) Utility medical / evacuation / emergency physician (15:14:02 / 03 / 04)
(These can map differently depending on your dictionary; shapes below reflect common roles.)

16A) Utility medical (15:14:02) – “medical support van/truck”
SILHOUETTE
- Front: van/truck cab.
- Rear: tall boxy medical module, shorter than a full ambulance.

HULL BLOCKS
1) Short hood wedge.
2) Cab box.
3) Medical module box:
   - A tall rectangular prism behind the cab (shorter than the cab+module of a full ambulance).

CUES
- Looks like “service truck with a box module”, not just a civilian van.

16B) Utility evacuation (15:14:03) – “ambulance-like”
SILHOUETTE
- Clear ambulance pattern: cab + large tall rear patient box.

HULL BLOCKS
1) Hood wedge (or cab-forward nose).
2) Cab box.
3) Patient module box:
   - Tall long rectangular prism; dominates rear ~55–70% of vehicle length.
4) Optional roof cap:
   - Thin box on top rear half (HVAC/roof equipment) if you need class separation.

CUES
- Rear module is big and tall; stronger ambulance silhouette than 16A.

16C) Utility emergency physician (15:14:04) – “rapid response car/SUV”
SILHOUETTE
- Essentially a sedan/SUV silhouette with small roof light bar.

HULL BLOCKS
1) Hood wedge.
2) Cabin/cargo box (SUV-ish) OR cabin + trunk (sedan-ish) depending on your preference.
3) Roof light bar block:
   - Very thin, very short box sitting centered on the roof (keep subtle).

CUES
- Only minimal “emergency” geometry: tiny roof light bar; otherwise looks like a normal vehicle.

=====================================================================
17) Law-enforcement / police vehicle baseline (15:17:07)

SILHOUETTE
- Typical patrol car: sedan-ish or SUV-ish, with a roof light bar.
- Side: clean civilian profile; minimal extra protrusions.

HULL BLOCKS
Option A (sedan patrol):
1) Hood wedge.
2) Cabin box.
3) Trunk box.
4) Roof light bar (thin small box centered on roof).

Option B (SUV patrol):
1) Hood wedge.
2) Cabin/cargo box.
3) Roof light bar (thin small box).

DISTINCT CUES
- Light bar is the key differentiator; otherwise it’s a normal car/SUV silhouette.

=====================================================================
18) Emergency ambulance / fire classes (15:23:01 / 02)

18A) Ambulance (15:23:01) – “Type III box ambulance” silhouette
SILHOUETTE
- Front: van/truck cab front.
- Rear: tall rectangular patient module; box dominates the vehicle.

HULL BLOCKS
1) Hood wedge / cab nose.
2) Cab box.
3) Patient module box (tall, long; rear ~60–75% of length).
4) Optional roof equipment strip (thin box) on top of module for extra identity.

CUES
- Strong cab+box pattern; module is taller and more pronounced than generic utility medical.

18B) Fire engine (15:23:02) – “pumper” silhouette
SILHOUETTE
- Front: truck cab.
- Middle: long equipment body with compartment faces.
- Rear/top: raised hose-bed block (a distinct high rear cap).

HULL BLOCKS
1) Hood wedge.
2) Cab box (tall).
3) Equipment body box:
   - Long rectangular prism behind cab (dominant middle volume).
4) Hose-bed / rear cap box:
   - A taller box sitting on the rear third of the equipment body (creates the classic stepped profile).
5) Optional “pump panel” wedge:
   - Small wedge/box on the side-mid area (only if you need one more unique cue; otherwise skip).

CUES
- Classic stepped silhouette: cab + long body + raised rear/top section.
- Much taller and more “industrial” than any civilian box truck.
